Albumin Corrected Anion Gap Calculator

Albumin-Corrected AG

Open in PediAid → ← All tools

1 What is the Albumin Corrected Anion Gap Calculator?

The Albumin Corrected Anion Gap Calculator adjusts a measured anion gap for low serum albumin, since hypoalbuminaemia lowers the anion gap and can mask an underlying high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis. It applies AG + 2.5 × (Normal − Measured Albumin) when albumin is in g/dL, or the equivalent AG + 0.25 × (Normal − Measured) in g/L. The app attributes the original derivation of this correction factor to Figge et al., Critical Care Medicine 1998.

2 When to use it

  • Recalculating the anion gap in hypoalbuminaemic patients (e.g. nephrotic syndrome, critical illness, malnutrition) where the raw AG may look falsely normal
  • Uncovering a masked high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis in critically ill children with low albumin
  • Reassessing acid-base status in patients with liver disease or protein-losing states where albumin is low
  • Complementing the standard Anion Gap Calculator whenever albumin is known to be abnormal
  • Supporting more accurate acid-base interpretation before pursuing a toxic-ingestion or metabolic work-up

3 Formula & method

Corrected AG = AG + 2.5 × (Normal Albumin − Measured Albumin) when albumin is entered in g/dL, or AG + 0.25 × (Normal − Measured) when entered in g/L — the app treats the two as equivalent. The calculator defaults 'Normal Albumin' to 4.0 g/dL (40 g/L), adjustable by the clinician. Per the app's insight card: 'Albumin is the dominant unmeasured anion contributing to a normal AG. For every 1 g/dL drop in albumin, the AG falls by ~2.5 mEq/L.'
  • Figge J, Crit Care Med 1998

4 Frequently asked questions

Why does albumin affect the anion gap?

The app explains that albumin is the dominant unmeasured anion contributing to a normal anion gap, so for every 1 g/dL drop in albumin, the measured AG falls by approximately 2.5 mEq/L.

What formula does this calculator use?

AG + 2.5 × (Normal Albumin − Measured Albumin) when albumin is entered in g/dL, or AG + 0.25 × (Normal − Measured) when entered in g/L.

What is the default 'normal' albumin value?

The calculator defaults to a normal albumin of 4.0 g/dL (or 40 g/L), which can be adjusted.

Who should use the corrected anion gap instead of the raw anion gap?

The app notes this is particularly relevant for critically ill or nephrotic patients, where the raw measured AG can mask a true high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis.

Where does the correction factor come from?

The app cites the original derivation to Figge J, Critical Care Medicine 1998.
