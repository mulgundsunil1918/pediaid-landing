Ependymoma — Toronto Stage

Staged by the presence of metastatic disease.

Open in PediAid → ← All tools

1 What is the Ependymoma — Toronto Stage?

The Ependymoma — Toronto Stage is a oncology scoring tool used in paediatric practice. Staged by the presence of metastatic disease. It scores 1 criteria and interprets the total across 2 bands, so the number always arrives with the severity it implies rather than on its own. In PediAid each criterion is shown as a labelled option with the weight it carries, so the total can be checked rather than taken on trust.

2 When to use it

  • Grading severity at the bedside in oncology
  • Recording an objective severity band in the case notes
  • Deciding when a total crosses the threshold for escalation
  • Re-scoring to track whether a child is improving or deteriorating
  • Checking the exact criteria of a score used infrequently

3 Formula & method

The score sums 1 criteria: Metastatic status at diagnosis. Totals are then banded: 1 stage or more - Stage I: localised ependymoma. Extent of resection is the strongest prognostic factor - aim for gross total resection followed by focal radiotherapy; 2 stage or more - Stage II: metastatic ependymoma - craniospinal radiotherapy and chemotherapy; significantly poorer prognosis.
  • Source: Gupta S, Aitken JF, Bartels U et al. Paediatric cancer stage in population-based cancer registries: the Toronto consensus principles and guidelines. Lancet Oncol 2016;17:e163-72.

4 Frequently asked questions

How many criteria does the Ependymoma — Toronto Stage use?

1. They are Metastatic status at diagnosis.

What do the bands of the Ependymoma — Toronto Stage mean?

The total is interpreted as: 1 stage or more - Stage I: localised ependymoma. Extent of resection is the strongest prognostic factor - aim for gross total resection followed by focal radiotherapy; 2 stage or more - Stage II: metastatic ependymoma - craniospinal radiotherapy and chemotherapy; significantly poorer prognosis.

Does the score replace clinical judgement?

No. It is a structured way to record and communicate severity. A clinician concerned about a child should act on that concern regardless of the total.

Where does this score come from?

Source: Gupta S, Aitken JF, Bartels U et al. Paediatric cancer stage in population-based cancer registries: the Toronto consensus principles and guidelines. Lancet Oncol 2016;17:e163-72.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Score definitions are bundled with the PediAid app, so the score computes without a connection.
