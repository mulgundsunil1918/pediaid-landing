UVC UAC Depth Calculator

Umbilical Catheter Insertion Length

Open in PediAid → ← All tools

1 What is the UVC UAC Depth Calculator?

The UVC/UAC Depth Calculator estimates insertion depth for umbilical venous and umbilical arterial catheters from birth weight, using the Shukla/Dunn formulas plus an adjustment for umbilical stump length. It reports separate depth estimates and target anatomical positions for the UVC (venous) and UAC (arterial, with weight-banded low/high-line options), alongside bedside best-practice guidance on catheter positioning and dwell time. The tool is built for neonatal clinicians placing or checking umbilical lines and is explicitly intended for use alongside X-ray confirmation.

2 When to use it

  • Estimating UVC insertion depth before placement in a neonate requiring central venous access
  • Estimating UAC insertion depth for arterial access or monitoring in a sick neonate
  • Cross-checking catheter depth against birth weight and umbilical stump length at the bedside
  • Anticipating expected tip position (e.g. T8–T9 for UVC) before reading a confirmatory X-ray
  • Reviewing best-practice timing for catheter replacement or removal to reduce infection or thrombosis risk

3 Formula & method

UVC depth (cm) = (3 × birth weight in kg + 9) / 2, plus umbilical stump length; target tip position T8–T9, 0.5–1.0 cm above the right diaphragm. UAC depth (cm): for birth weight ≥ 1500 g, (3 × BW kg) + 9, plus stump length; for birth weight < 1500 g, (4 × BW kg) + 7, plus stump length; target position is Low (L3–L4) or High (T6–T9). The source file also references two research add-on methods not exposed as separate inputs: the Wright birth-length method (UVC = 0.5 × shoulder–umbilicus length + 1) and a Dunn surface-length method using crown–heel length.

4 Frequently asked questions

What formula does the app use for UVC depth?

(3 × birth weight in kg + 9) / 2, plus the umbilical stump length, targeting a tip position at T8–T9.

Does the UAC formula change with birth weight?

Yes — the app uses (3 × BW kg) + 9 for babies ≥ 1500 g and (4 × BW kg) + 7 for babies < 1500 g, both plus stump length.

Why does umbilical stump length matter in this calculation?

The calculator adds the stump length to the calculated depth because the underlying formulas are referenced from the abdominal wall, not the cut end of the cord.

Is a high or low UAC line preferred?

The app's best-practice notes state a high UAC (T6–T9) carries lower thrombosis/vascular compromise risk than a low UAC (L3–L4) and is preferred where practicable.

How is catheter position confirmed after placement?

The app specifies confirming both lines on an AP chest/abdominal X-ray before infusing inotropes or hyperosmolar fluid.
