Ponderal Index Calculator

IUGR & Nutritional Status

Open in PediAid → ← All tools

1 What is the Ponderal Index Calculator?

The Ponderal Index (PI) Calculator assesses a newborn's nutritional status and body proportionality from birth weight and length. As described in the app, it distinguishes between symmetrical and asymmetrical intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) — a distinction that birth-weight percentiles alone cannot make.

2 When to use it

  • Evaluating a newborn with a birth weight suggestive of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)
  • Distinguishing symmetrical (proportionate) from asymmetrical (disproportionate) growth restriction
  • Assessing body proportionality and nutritional status at birth
  • Complementing birth-weight-for-gestational-age percentiles with a proportionality measure
  • Flagging newborns who may need closer nutritional or growth follow-up

3 Formula & method

PI = (Weight in grams / Length in cm³) × 100, as shown in the app. The result is categorized into three bands per the app's reference table: PI above 2.5 — AGA/Normal, consistent with an appropriate-for-gestational-age term newborn; PI 2.0–2.5 — Symmetrical IUGR, where both weight and length are proportionally reduced, indicating chronic early-onset growth restriction; PI below 2.0 — Asymmetrical IUGR, described in the app as critically low and 'consistent with Asymmetrical IUGR and Severe PEM,' warranting immediate clinical evaluation.

4 Frequently asked questions

What is the Ponderal Index formula?

PI = (Weight in grams ÷ Length in cm³) × 100, per the app.

What does a Ponderal Index below 2.0 mean?

The app labels this 'Asymmetrical IUGR,' describing it as critically low and consistent with severe protein-energy malnutrition (PEM), warranting immediate clinical evaluation.

How is symmetrical IUGR different from asymmetrical IUGR on this scale?

A PI of 2.0–2.5 is categorized as symmetrical IUGR, where weight and length are both proportionally reduced (chronic, early-onset restriction); below 2.0 is asymmetrical IUGR, where weight is disproportionately low relative to length.

What inputs does the calculator need?

Birth weight in grams (validated range 500–6000 g) and birth length in cm (validated range 25–65 cm).
