Estimated Blood Volume Calculator

EBV by Age & Weight

Open in PediAid → ← All calculators

1 What is the Estimated Blood Volume Calculator?

The Estimated Blood Volume Calculator computes a patient's total circulating blood volume from body weight using age-specific mL/kg values, spanning preterm neonates through adolescents/adults. Alongside the estimated blood volume (EBV), it surfaces bedside transfusion thresholds — a 10% single-draw/transfusion-start volume and a 15% haemorrhagic-shock threshold — plus the maximum allowable blood loss (MABL) formula. It is intended as a quick reference for clinicians managing phlebotomy limits, transfusion planning, or perioperative blood loss in neonates and children.

2 When to use it

  • Estimating safe single-draw or diagnostic phlebotomy volumes in neonates and small infants
  • Planning transfusion volumes or thresholds in a bleeding or anaemic child
  • Perioperative or trauma assessment of allowable blood loss before transfusion is required
  • Contextualising acute blood loss as a percentage of a patient's total blood volume
  • Quick bedside lookup when a patient's expected mL/kg band isn't immediately known

3 Formula & method

EBV = weight (kg) × mL/kg, where mL/kg is selected by age band: Preterm neonate 95 mL/kg, Term neonate (0–1 month) 85 mL/kg, Infant (1–12 months) 80 mL/kg, Child (1–12 years) 70 mL/kg, Adolescent/Adult (>12 years) 65 mL/kg. The app also derives 10% of EBV (safe single-draw/starting transfusion volume), 15% of EBV (haemorrhagic shock threshold), and Maximum Allowable Blood Loss: MABL = EBV × (Hct₀ − Hct_min) / Hct_avg.
  • Fluid and Electrolyte Formulae
  • Coté & Lerman, Practice of Anesthesia for Infants and Children, 6th ed.

4 Frequently asked questions

What mL/kg value does the app use for a term newborn?

85 mL/kg, per the Term neonate row in the app's age-band table.

How does the blood volume estimate change with age?

The app steps mL/kg down as children get older — 95 for preterm neonates, 85 for term neonates, 80 for infants, 70 for children, and 65 for adolescents/adults.

What does the 15% threshold mean?

The app flags 15% of estimated blood volume as the haemorrhagic shock threshold, at which blood should be prepared.

What is MABL?

Maximum Allowable Blood Loss, calculated in the app as EBV × (Hct₀ − Hct_min) / Hct_avg, used to estimate how much blood can be lost before transfusion is indicated.

Is the mL/kg figure exact for every patient?

The app notes that the patient ranges shown are typical and should be verified against the individual patient's clinical context.
